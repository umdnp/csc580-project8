# Threats to validity {#sec-validity}

**Construct validity.** The eleven RIs cover selected observable changes, not every security risk. Pattern-based rules can mistake examples or warnings for operations, and can miss unfamiliar syntax, dynamically constructed destinations, indirect commands, or other unrecognized behavior. They may also miss risky instructions written only in non-English prose when no recognizable command, URL, path, or credential term appears. We include non-English artifacts, but exclude `SKILL.md` YAML frontmatter from behavioral scanning; instructions found only in that metadata can therefore be missed. The rules also do not separately detect environment discovery, such as reading operating-system details. Complete extraction means a recognized construct was captured, not that all relevant behavior was found.

**Relatedness and historical order.** Identical names can group unrelated skills; renamed relatives can be missed. Token-shingle containment and Jaccard support relatedness but do not prove copying, and similarity clusters do not identify verified original authors or sources. GitSkills does not supply complete historical contents. Commit dates refer to observations at current file paths; equivalent-peer chronology, repository-creation boundaries, and containment extend coverage but remain indirect. **566 of 2,157** directed comparisons use containment as their basis, including **131 of 340** comparisons with supported RIs. Their inferred order needs particular caution.

**Missing evidence.** Some sibling files have no stored text, and historical metadata is uneven. Missing dates or content can prevent comparisons or hide behavior, potentially favoring better-documented artifacts. The three trials report **211 comparisons with incomplete evidence coverage**, including **81** with supported indicators. An indicator can have complete local evidence even when another part of its comparison is missing. Unknown sibling state is not an empty or unchanged bundle; conversely, a comparison marked complete may still contain behavior outside our rules.

**Sampling and generalizability.** Each trial used the 20/55/20/4/1 family-size allocation and required at least one usable primary directed relationship. Larger families were deliberately oversampled, and ineligible candidates were excluded rather than counted as having no findings. Later trials excluded names already selected, so the three samples are non-overlapping but not independent draws from an unchanged pool. Neither the **21.0% family rate** nor the **15.8% comparison rate** estimates prevalence in all GitSkills families, much less all agent skills. The dataset covers a particular release of publicly collected GitHub skills, not every platform or private repository. Family size counts occurrences, not independent authors or changes.

**Dependence and conclusion validity.** Multiple comparisons may share artifacts or the same underlying change: `mcp-builder`, for example, accounts for **133 of 191** indicator-bearing comparisons in Trial 3. Even across different families, shared templates may produce related findings, so family-level counts do not guarantee independent observations. We analyzed primary predecessor edges only; other related edges may contain changes we did not assess. The sensitivity check measures the effect of dominant families, not population prevalence or the outcomes of an all-edge analysis.

**Validation and reproducibility.** The three trials generated **90 directed** and **45 relationship-review cases**, but human adjudication is still in progress. Automated results and AI-assisted notes do not establish precision, recall, or relationship agreement. Manifests preserve the data identity, code revision, rules, sampling seeds, and case checksums; numeric artifact IDs are local to a DuckDB build. The saved manifests still mark approval as provisional. These safeguards aid reproducibility but do not remove selection bias or detection errors.

The standalone `THREAT_MODEL.md` and `THREATS_TO_VALIDITY.md` provide further detail on detection coverage, safe handling, and alternative explanations.
