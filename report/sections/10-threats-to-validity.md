# Threats to validity

Our findings describe security-sensitive changes identified by a fixed analysis method in selected GitSkills families. Interpreting those findings depends on what the scanner can recognize, how reliably the artifacts can be compared, and which families entered the study. Each limitation affects the conclusions we can draw from the automated results.

**Detection and interpretation (construct validity).** We use 15 text-based rules and 11 risk indicators to identify changes worth reviewing, not to recognize every possible attack. Examples and warnings can resemble instructions to execute, while indirect commands, dynamically constructed network destinations, and instructions expressed only in non-English prose may go undetected. Moreover, an accurately detected operation may reflect legitimate maintenance rather than malicious intent or increased risk.

Initial manual reviews illustrate these limits. In two Trial 3 `mcp-builder` comparisons, a reviewer questioned RI-003 because hostnames reported as new were already present in the base artifacts. Trial 1 notes identify potentially missed indicators, and Trial 3 notes identify operations in sibling files that may have been overlooked. Additional feedback suggests that RI-010 may miss a local script invoked as `scripts/...` instead of `./scripts/...`. Browser launches using `open` or `xdg-open` with an HTTP(S) URL raise a different question: whether navigation belongs within RI-003's intended definition of a network operation. These observations are not adjudicated errors; each requires the full comparison and indicator criteria to be checked.

**Relatedness and historical order (internal validity).** To say an operation was introduced, we must first identify related artifacts and choose a plausible earlier-to-later order. Grouping by exact skill name can include unrelated artifacts and miss renamed ones; our similarity thresholds can also exclude related content. A Trial 3 reviewer judged three of five sampled `no-related-edge` pairs to be related, suggesting a possible gap but not establishing its frequency. Even when artifacts are related, GitSkills does not provide verified copying histories. We use available provenance, chronology, and content evidence to orient comparisons. Directional containment, the weakest basis, was used for 566 of 2,157 primary comparisons, including 131 of the 340 with supported indicators. These are inferred comparison directions, not proven historical sequences.

**Missing evidence.** Some supporting files lack stored content, and historical metadata is incomplete. This may hide relevant behavior or prevent a relationship from being evaluated, favoring artifacts with better available records. Of 2,157 primary comparisons, 211 had incomplete evidence coverage, including 81 of the 340 with supported indicators. A bundle with unknown sibling-file content is not necessarily empty or unchanged. Likewise, a comparison marked complete has no known gap within the implemented extraction scope; that does not mean every security-sensitive operation was detected.

**Sampling and generalizability (external validity).** Each of the three trials selected 100 families using a 20/55/20/4/1 size-group allocation that deliberately oversampled larger families. Families without a usable primary directed relationship were excluded, and later trials excluded names chosen earlier. The trials are therefore non-overlapping, but not independent draws from an unchanged population. The automated 21.0% family-level and 15.8% comparison-level rates describe these selected families and pairs. They do not estimate the frequency of such changes across all GitSkills, other skill sources, or confirmed security incidents.

**Repeated evidence and edge selection (conclusion validity).** Several comparisons can capture the same underlying change. For example, `mcp-builder` contributed 133 of the 191 indicator-bearing comparisons in Trial 3. Reporting families as the primary unit reduces that concentration, although shared templates can still create dependence across families. We scanned only selected primary predecessor edges, so other related comparisons may contain changes we did not assess. Our sensitivity check shows the effect of dominant families; it does not resolve those broader limitations.

**Validation and reproducibility.** All 90 directed and 45 relationship-review records are marked `complete`, but 50 directed cases are `not_assessable` for indicator review, and final team adjudication is not documented ([Appendix C](#app-c)). The notes help identify possible errors, but do not yet support finalized precision, recall, reviewer agreement, or corrected detection rates. The Sprint 3 scanner and automated results remain fixed under the recorded configuration. Saved manifests preserve the dataset identity, code revision, rules, seeds, and review checksums, although numeric artifact IDs can differ between database builds and sample approval remains provisional. This supports reproduction of the reported analysis, not independent verification of every finding.

The separate threat model and threats-to-validity documents provide further details on these limitations.
