# Background and related work {#sec-related-work}

Relevant agent-skill security studies ask what risky instructions a skill *contains*. Liu and colleagues developed SkillScan to identify patterns involving data exposure, privilege escalation, and supply-chain concerns [@skillswild2026]. Another study combined static screening with behavioral verification to investigate malicious skills distributed through community registries [@maliciousskills2026]. These studies show why skill contents deserve security review. We focus instead on **what security-sensitive operations appear or change between related artifacts**. Their reported detection rates are not directly comparable with ours because the datasets and criteria differ.

Other research examines how skills change over time. Huang and colleagues studied GitHub commit histories to understand which skill components are modified and why [@skillevolution2026]. Our dataset does not contain a complete commit history for each skill, so we instead compare content found in different repositories. Similarity and available historical clues help us choose plausible earlier/later comparisons, but they cannot prove copying or recover an original author.

Our contribution combines these two perspectives. We identify related skill content, record the evidence behind each comparison's direction, and assess whether the observed differences satisfy specific security-sensitive change criteria. The result is a set of candidate changes for human review, not a list of confirmed malicious skills.
